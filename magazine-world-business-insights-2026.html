Her story is not simply about establishing organisations, building networks or navigating entrepreneurship. According to her personal faith testimony, the companies came only after a prolonged process of spiritual preparation that she describes as the Seven Purifications — THE 7 SQUEEZES OF GOD. She is emphatic about the motive: “I HAD ZERO PERSONAL AMBITION TO ESTABLISH THESE COMPANIES. I STILL HAVE ZERO PERSONAL AMBITION. MY DAILY AIM IS TO DO THE CREATOR’S WILL.” In her account, enterprise was never the destination. It became an instrument placed in her hands for a larger mission.

Behind the growth was something more fundamental: a willingness to listen, respond and serve.

For SHEKINAIH, every problem represents more than an obstacle. It can become an invitation to understand. What caused the difficulty? What needs to be corrected? What has been overlooked? This way of thinking reflects the empathy at the heart of her philosophy. Rather than seeing people merely as customers, partners or stakeholders, she speaks of humanity with a sense of responsibility. Problems are not abstract business cases; they affect real people, families, communities and nations. Solutions, therefore, must carry human meaning.

Her imagination extends beyond conventional definitions of innovation. She describes a desire to pursue what she calls “divine innovation” ideas and possibilities that, in her understanding, originate beyond ordinary human limitations. This conviction has shaped the way she approaches leadership: not simply asking what is commercially possible, but what might become possible when creativity, wisdom and purpose are brought together.

According to her personal testimony, the defining sequence began before the companies existed. She says THE GOD OF THE UNIVERSE first took her through Seven Purifications — THE 7 SQUEEZES OF GOD — to prepare her for the responsibility ahead. Only after that preparation did she receive the instruction to establish CROWNED EAGLES GLOBAL LLP and CE GLOBAL NETWORK LLP. CE Global Network LLP later evolved through restructuring into CE Global Network International Pte. Ltd. For her, this order is essential: preparation before platform, obedience before enterprise, and purpose before growth.

Today, her leadership spans Singapore and Malaysia, with responsibilities across Crowned Eagles Global and the CE Global Network ecosystem. Yet the titles and corporate structures tell only one part of the story. The deeper narrative is about becoming a vessel prepared to serve a purpose.

SHEKINAIH repeatedly returns to the language of restoration. She speaks of moving from limitation into limitless possibility, from earthward thinking into what she describes as heavenward revelation, and from impossibility into divine possibility. These expressions reveal an entrepreneurial worldview in which business is not separated from personal transformation.

In that worldview, leadership begins with the willingness to examine oneself before attempting to change the world. Repentance, for example, is not presented merely as an abstract spiritual concept. She asks what should follow it: correction, responsibility, changed behaviour and tangible fruit. When something has gone wrong, the objective is not simply to identify blame. It is to understand the root cause, undo what must be undone, repair what can be repaired and create conditions for a better outcome.

That approach carries an important lesson for leadership. Restoration requires more than intention. It requires action. It requires the humility to acknowledge what is broken, the empathy to understand who has been affected, the imagination to envision something better, and the courage to take the steps necessary to move toward it. Her greatest joy, she says, is to become a vessel through whom the God of the Universe can flow toward restoration to what she understands as the pre-fall and ultimately perfected state.

Whether one encounters her story through the lens of entrepreneurship, leadership, faith or social impact, its central theme remains consistent: possibility begins when limitation is no longer accepted as the final answer. This is the philosophy behind her aspiration to impart what she believes has been entrusted to her wisdom, understanding,

It is also what makes her journey distinctive.

SHEKINAIH is not merely building organisations. She is pursuing a vision of what organisations can become when they are guided by imagination, shaped by empathy and grounded in respect for the people they are intended to serve.

Her story ultimately asks a larger question: What if every challenge contains the possibility of restoration and every limitation is an invitation to imagine something greater?

2. From Earthward Thinking — Into Heavenward Revelation.

Where Discipline Met Compassion, and Faith Became a Way of Leadership

Her journey did not begin with a title, an award, or a magazine cover. It began with discipline, service, and a series of choices that gradually taught her that leadership is not about how high one rises, but about what one carries for others.

Born in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, SHEKINAIH later earned a Bachelor of Business Studies in Accounting and Finance from Massey University, New Zealand. Her early professional formation included audit work with Deloitte, followed by approximately six and a half years in financial control and corporate banking at United Overseas Bank (UOB) in Singapore. These experiences trained her to examine facts carefully, understand risk, protect financial integrity, interpret the story behind numbers, and make disciplined decisions.

Yet, the education that most profoundly shaped her heart took place beyond the boardroom. For approximately ten years, she dedicated herself substantially to humanitarian and ministry service, praying for and serving people recovering from drug addiction in a halfway house, people who were blind, persons with intellectual disabilities, elderly residents in care homes, and families carrying burdens that could not be resolved through motivational

Page 10

words alone. One experience became a defining test of her beliefs.

She took responsibility for a fatherless nineteen-year-old young man whose family was experiencing significant hardship. Following what she understood as divine instruction, she paid for his university education, helped his family obtain a home, provided food for them for six years, and, after he graduated in computer science, used her savings to purchase a tuition centre where he could teach, earn an income, and support his family. That act of obedience occurred on the very day her husband was retrenched.

What followed was a six-year period she describes as “closed heaven.” Her husband submitted more than 200 job applications, including applications for positions offering as little as SGD 2,000, yet no offer came. Through that prolonged uncertainty, SHEKINAIH says she continued to trust THE GOD OF THE UNIVERSE to sustain their family.

When their bank balance was approaching zero, she prayed that it was time for Heaven to open. Within approximately two weeks, a headhunter contacted her husband. Although other suitable candidates were available, the headhunter believed the role was meant for him. He ultimately received the position at remuneration equivalent to his previous salary.

For SHEKINAIH, the experience became more than a personal testimony. It became a principle that continues to influence the way she understands adversity, faith and leadership: “Obedience may cost you before it rewards you. Faith may be tested before breakthrough appears. Do not interpret delay as abandonment.” That season forged her worldview.

Accounting taught her accountability. Banking taught her systems. Humanitarian service taught her compassion. Adversity taught her endurance. Faith taught her to look Heavenward when horizontal answers were exhausted. Over time, her understanding of success changed. Life became less about pursuing achievement for its own sake and more about pursuing alignment with purpose. For SHEKINAIH, leadership therefore became something carried rather than something claimed a responsibility to use knowledge, resources, influence and opportunity in ways that can create meaningful change in the lives of others.

The greatest turning point in SHEKINAIH’s journey did not begin with a business opportunity. According to her personal testimony, it began with preparation. She describes being purified seven times — a process she calls THE 7 SQUEEZES OF GOD — before she was ready to carry the responsibility that followed. Only after those Seven Purifications, she says, did THE GOD OF THE UNIVERSE instruct her to establish CROWNED EAGLES GLOBAL LLP and CE GLOBAL NETWORK LLP. CE Global Network LLP later evolved through restructuring into CE Global Network International Pte. Ltd. She is clear that the sequence matters: purification first, instruction second, enterprise third. “THE COMPANIES DID NOT BEGIN WITH MY AMBITION. THEY BEGAN WITH AN INSTRUCTION. MY RESPONSIBILITY IS TO OBEY, STEWARD AND FULFIL THE PURPOSE FOR WHICH THEY WERE GIVEN.”

From that instruction onward, enterprise became an instrument rather than an identity. Business became an instrument. Capital became an instrument. Leadership became an instrument. Intellectual property became an instrument. Publishing became an instrument. Land became an instrument. Influence became an instrument. Humanitarian service became an instrument. In her words: “ENTERPRISE IS THE INSTRUMENT. THE SEVEN MISSIONS ARE THE REASON.” This perspective shapes the way she approaches strategy, governance and decision-making: discernment before action, purpose before performance, stewardship before ownership and obedience before expansion.

According to her testimony, THE 7 SQUEEZES OF GOD and the Seven Purifications came first. Only after that preparation did THE GOD OF THE UNIVERSE instruct her to establish the organisations. She describes their origin as an assignment carrying Seven Missions through CROWNED EAGLES GLOBAL HEAVEN THRONE ROOM MINISTRY. The business ecosystem therefore exists to serve the mission, not to replace it.

In her understanding, the business ecosystem exists to serve that mission rather than replace it. Enterprise

1. A Bridge Between Heaven and Earth
The first purpose is to stand as what she describes as a bridge between Heaven and Earth interceding for individuals, families, communities, cities, countries and nations.

For SHEKINAIH, intercession represents an act of responsibility and compassion: carrying the concerns of others before God and seeking what she understands as divine guidance for their circumstances.

2. Bringing Heaven’s Answers to Humanity
The second purpose is to bring what she understands as Heaven’s answers and solutions to humanity.

These concerns extend across individuals and families to businesses, communities, governments and nations. Her vision is rooted in the belief that seemingly complex human challenges can be approached through wisdom, discernment and solutions that transcend conventional thinking.

3. Alerting and Preparing Humanity
The third purpose is centred on warning, preparation and equipping. SHEKINAIH describes a responsibility to alert people to potential dangers, disruptions and circumstances so that they may be avoided, reduced or overcome. Preparation, in this context, becomes an expression of care helping people respond to uncertainty with greater awareness and readiness.

4. Communicating Spiritual Revelation Concerning Leaders and Nations
The fourth purpose concerns what she describes as Heaven’s decrees concerning leaders, nations and world events. She presents these not as conventional political forecasts, but as matters of spiritual revelation and personal faith testimony. The distinction is important to her worldview: the emphasis is on discernment, prayer and spiritual understanding rather than political prediction.

5. Healing, Miracles and Restoration
The fifth purpose is to minister healing, miracles and restoration. Her understanding of restoration extends beyond physical wellbeing. It encompasses the physical, emotional, spiritual, relational and total-life dimensions of a person's experience. 6. Addressing Humanity’s Seemingly Unsolvable Problems
The sixth purpose is perhaps the broadest: confronting problems that appear impossible to resolve. These may be personal, spiritual, physical, financial, organisational, governmental, national or international in nature. SHEKINAIH's approach begins with asking deeper questions: What is the root cause? What needs to change? What must be restored? What steps are required? And how can a negative circumstance ultimately be transformed into a positive outcome?

This reflects her belief that limitations should not automatically be treated as final destinations.

7. Serving as Destiny Impartors
The seventh purpose is what SHEKINAIH describes as serving as destiny imparters. The objective is to help individuals, families, communities, cities and nations discover and fulfil what she describes as their original divine purpose, design and destiny. In this understanding, leadership is not about controlling another person's journey. It is about helping people recognise their potential, understand their purpose and move toward the possibilities they believe have been placed before them.

Money, companies, influence, knowledge, time, relationships, property and opportunity are not viewed merely as possessions. Faith Must Become Structure

For SHEKINAIH, spiritual conviction is not an alternative to sound governance. In fact, she believes it should demand greater accountability. Faith, in her philosophy, cannot become an excuse for disorder. Purpose must become structure. Vision must become execution. Compassion must become measurable action. Prayer must be accompanied by responsibility. This principle creates an important bridge between her spiritual worldview and her professional approach. A compelling vision may provide direction, but systems, governance, discipline and execution are required to translate that vision into reality.

Her philosophy can therefore be expressed through a simple progression: Faith → Purpose → Structure → Action → Stewardship → Impact

It is this progression that allows her to view enterprise as more than an economic mechanism. Business becomes a vehicle through which responsibility can be organised, resources can be deployed and long-term objectives can be pursued.

The Discipline of Discernment

For SHEKINAIH, every opportunity must be evaluated against more than its financial potential. She approaches major decisions through a series of filters: purpose, stewardship, integrity, sustainability, strategic alignment, people impact, professional diligence and discernment. An opportunity may appear highly profitable and still be incompatible with the mission. A risk may appear intimidating and yet be necessary to pursue. A deal may look attractive on the surface but carry a hidden cost that is ultimately too high if it compromises integrity.

This distinction is central to her philosophy of leadership: not every profitable opportunity is a purposeful opportunity. SHEKINAIH also draws a clear line between courageous risk and reckless risk. Courage, in her view, does not mean ignoring uncertainty. It means confronting uncertainty with preparation. Before making a significant decision, she believes leaders should understand the numbers, examine the legal implications, consider taxation, review contracts, assess cash flow, understand operational requirements and measure downside exposure. Professional advice should be sought where necessary.

Only after the practical dimensions have been examined does she bring prayer and discernment into the decision-making process. Understand. Examine. Verify. Seek counsel. Pray. Discern. Act. This approach reflects her belief that faith and professional diligence should reinforce rather than contradict one another. Spiritual conviction, in her view, should encourage greater responsibility not less.

Breaking the Codes of Conventional Leadership

For SHEKINAIH, the future of leadership requires more than new strategies, technologies or business models. It requires challenging the assumptions that have shaped leadership for generations.

1. Leadership Equals Power: The first outdated assumption she seeks to challenge is that leadership equals power. In her philosophy, leadership is better understood as stewardship. Authority is not simply a privilege to direct others; it is a responsibility to protect, develop and create value for those entrusted to a leader's care.

2. People Exist to Serve Organisation: The second assumption is that people exist primarily to serve an organisation. She believes the relationship must work both ways. Organisations depend on people to achieve their objectives, but healthy organisations must also develop the people who build them. Growth should therefore be measured not only through revenue, market position or expansion, but also through the capability, confidence and well being of the people within the organisation.

3. Profit: The third assumption concerns profit. She does not dismiss profitability. On the contrary, she recognises that profit is essential for sustainability and the ability to continue operating. But she questions the idea that maximum profit should be the ultimate measure of excellence. In her view, profit without purpose can create organisations that are powerful on paper but empty at their core. Sustainable excellence requires a connection between financial performance and a larger sense of responsibility.

4. Leadership: The fourth assumption she challenges is that conscience, spirituality and meaning have no place in serious leadership. For SHEKINAIH, leaders are human beings leading human beings. Decisions inevitably carry values, consequences and meaning. Technical competence may determine whether a strategy works, but values influence what leaders choose to pursue in the first place. Identity matters. Conscience matters. Values matter. Rather than treating these dimensions as weaknesses, she views them as part of responsible leadership.

5. Hidden Uncertainty: The fifth assumption is that uncertainty must always be hidden. She believes strong leaders do not need to pretend that they possess every answer. Instead, strength can be demonstrated through the humility to acknowledge uncertainty, seek wisdom, consult others and make responsible decisions with the information available. For her, wisdom is not the absence of uncertainty; it is the ability to navigate uncertainty responsibly.

Breaking the Horizontal Mindset

Perhaps the deepest assumption SHEKINAIH wants to challenge is the belief that humanity must look only horizontally for answers. She recognises the extraordinary importance of human achievement. This conviction is central to her five-volume BEHOLD MY MESSENGER series, particularly BEHOLD MY MESSENGER 5: BEHOLD THE RESURRECTION OF THE DEAD. For SHEKINAIH, resurrection represents the possibility that something declared finished may not actually be finished.

A dream may appear dead.
A business may appear dead.
A destiny may appear dead.
Hope may appear dead.

Page 32

But she challenges people not to write the final sentence before what they understand as the Creator has spoken. Her message is therefore not limited to physical resurrection. It extends metaphorically and spiritually toward the resurrection of hope, purpose, destiny, vision, families, enterprises and nations. Within the testimonies associated with her ministry and writings, she shares accounts that she understands as expressions of restoration and resurrection power. She presents these as faith testimonies rather than substitutes for professional medical evidence or appropriate care. The deeper purpose, in her view, is to encourage people to recognise that what humanity calls “the end” may not necessarily be the final chapter.

The Mega Declaration

Her philosophy culminates in what she describes as a Final Mega Declaration to Humanity. She does not reject human capability. On the contrary, she acknowledges the extraordinary resources humanity has developed:

Science.
Medicine.
Technology.
Artificial intelligence.
Government.
Corporations.
Universities.
Capital.
Education.
Professional expertise.

Her instruction is to use them responsibly. Use medicine. Use doctors. Use technology. Use science. Use advisers. Use law. Use education. Use finance. Use human wisdom.

But, in her worldview: “BUT DO NOT STOP THERE.”

When human intelligence reaches what she perceives as its ceiling, she calls people to look beyond that ceiling.

WHEN STRATEGY FAILS — LOOK UP.
WHEN EVERY DOOR APPEARS CLOSED — LOOK UP.
WHEN THE PROBLEM APPEARS IMPOSSIBLE — LOOK UP.
WHEN THE BOARDROOM HAS NO ANSWER — LOOK UP.
WHEN THE NATION HAS NO ANSWER — LOOK UP.
WHEN HUMANITY HAS NO ANSWER — LOOK UP.

For SHEKINAIH, looking Heavenward does not mean abandoning human intelligence. It means combining human responsibility with what she understands as divine wisdom.

Instruction Must Become Action

Her philosophy ultimately follows a sequence: INSTRUCTION → OBEDIENCE → ACTION → BREAKTHROUGH

The sequence reflects her conviction that receiving what she understands as divine direction is only the beginning. Instruction must be followed by obedience, obedience by action, and action by the possibility of breakthrough. This principle also connects with her wider leadership philosophy.

Knowledge has value.
Strategy has value.
Innovation has value.
Resources have value.

But their greatest potential is realised when they are aligned with purpose. She therefore sees adversity differently from conventional definitions of failure. When knowledge reaches its limit, possibility may begin. When destiny appears delayed, preparation may be taking place. When adversity strikes, purpose can still prevail. When impossibility stands before humanity, possibility may still emerge. And when the world sees a grave, she believes Heaven may see a beginning.

From Limitation to Limitless

Everything returns to the central language of her philosophy:

FROM LIMITATION — INTO LIMITLESS.
FROM EARTHWARD THINKING — INTO HEAVENWARD REVELATION.
FROM IMPOSSIBILITY — INTO DIVINE POSSIBILITY.
FROM THE GRAVE — INTO RESURRECTION.

Her invitation to humanity is therefore expansive:

LOOK HEAVENWARD.
SEEK YOUR CREATOR.
RECEIVE YOUR HEAVEN BLUEPRINT.
RECEIVE YOUR DESTINY.
RECEIVE DIVINE WISDOM.
RECEIVE DIVINE STRATEGY.
RECEIVE DIVINE INNOVATION.

Page 33

And then:
RISE. RESTORE. RESURRECT. TRANSFORM. IMPART. PREPARE. PRESERVE. FULFIL DESTINY.

At the heart of SHEKINAIH's message is the belief that human limitation does not necessarily represent the final boundary of possibility. The end of human power, in her philosophy, does not have to be the end of the story.

“THE CREATOR IS STILL ABOVE THE CREATION.”

And that is ultimately where she wants her leadership to direct the gaze of the next generation not away from the world and its remarkable achievements, but beyond human limitation toward what she understands as the wisdom, purpose and possibility of Heaven.

BEHOLD THE RESURRECTION OF THE DEAD.

WHEN ALL ELSE FAILS, TRY THE GOD OF THE UNIVERSE IN SHEKINAIH! IT WORKS BECAUSE FULLY BACK UP BY THE POWER OF HEAVEN!!! BANG! BANG! BANG!
