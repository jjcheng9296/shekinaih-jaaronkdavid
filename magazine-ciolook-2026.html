Building Institutions with Purpose and Integrity

Every meaningful institution begins with a clear purpose and a commitment to values that stand the test of time. Across industries, lasting progress is shaped by people who combine professional excellence with integrity, education, and service to society. Their work reminds us that true achievement is measured not only by business results but also by the positive influence created for communities and future generations. Like a leader who successfully united enterprise, publishing, education, and humanitarian initiatives across multiple countries, purpose-driven action can create opportunities that extend far beyond individual success.

In this edition, Insights Success Magazine proudly presents “SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP: The Visionary Building Institutions for a Greater Purpose.” This edition celebrates the value of ethical leadership, thoughtful decision-making, and the determination to build organizations that create lasting value. It also highlights how vision, discipline, and character can inspire meaningful progress while encouraging others to pursue excellence with responsibility and conviction.

Our featured leader has built a career that reaches across business, education, publishing, and humanitarian initiatives with a clear sense of purpose. Every endeavor reflects a commitment to creating institutions that encourage learning, trust, and sustainable growth. By placing character and long-term thinking at the heart of every decision, this leader continues to influence professionals and communities across borders. The journey presented in these pages offers valuable lessons for anyone seeking to create a meaningful and lasting impact.

Hope this edition inspires you to pursue purposeful leadership, strengthen your values, and discover new possibilities for creating a positive difference in your professional and personal journey!

Managing Editor

SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP: THE VISIONARY BUILDING INSTITUTIONS FOR A GREATER PURPOSE

SHEKINAIH

THE SIGNATURE STORY

Page 12

Most business leaders begin with a commercial idea and search for meaning along the way. Fewer start with a sense of mission and build the commercial infrastructure around it afterward. That second path is considerably harder. It demands a different kind of discipline, a willingness to let purpose shape structure rather than the other way around, and the patience to build slowly and deliberately toward something larger than personal achievement. SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP, Principal Managing Partner at CE Global Network LLP, has followed exactly that path. Her leadership spans enterprise, publishing, teaching, and humanitarian work across Singapore, Malaysia, and international platforms, all shaped by a philosophy that places stewardship, character, and long-term purpose at the centre of every decision she makes.

Purpose as the Foundation of Enterprise

For many entrepreneurs, the journey begins with a business idea and develops through commercial ambition, growth and expansion. For SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP, the journey followed a different path. She does not describe her story as one where she first established a business and later discovered a greater purpose. Instead, she describes her personal faith journey and sense of spiritual calling as the starting point, with the business structures developing afterward as a means of carrying that purpose forward. “The instruction came first; the structure followed,” she says.

Today, SHEKINAIH SIEW SIN YAP is the Founder and Managing Partner of Crowned Eagles Global Network LLP and CE Global Network LLP. She is also the Founder and Managing Director of Crowned Eagles Global Group Holdings Pte. Ltd., CE Global Network International Pte. Ltd., Crowned Eagles Global IP Holdings Pte. Ltd., CEG Malaysia Holdings Sdn. Bhd. and CEG Portfolio Abundant Sdn. Bhd.

Her work brings together enterprise, leadership, publishing, teaching and humanitarian engagement, all connected by a deeply personal spiritual mission. At the centre of her philosophy is an understanding of enterprise that differs from conventional measures of business success. For her, “enterprise is not the destination. It is infrastructure.”

Obedience Before Ambition

SHEKINAIH’s professional background is grounded in business and finance. She holds a Bachelor of Business Studies, majoring in Accounting and Finance, from Massey University. She has also undertaken additional professional training and qualifications in areas connected with accounting, finance and real estate. Her professional and international engagement is further supported by her ability to work across English, Bahasa Malaysia and Bahasa Indonesia.

While these qualifications provide an important foundation for her approach to business and leadership, she identifies her personal faith journey as another defining influence. According to her personal faith testimony, the companies she leads emerged after a prolonged period of spiritual testing, purification and surrender. She refers to this experience in her teachings as the “Seven Squeezes of God.”

She says she had “zero personal ambition” to establish companies for status, recognition or personal empire-building. Instead, she describes her journey through the lens of obedience. Rather than asking how large she could become, she focused on what she needed to do next.

“My response was not, ‘How big can I become?’ It was, ‘What must I obey next?’” she explains.

This perspective has shaped the principles that guide her leadership. For her, leadership is built around obedience before ego, stewardship before status, and restoration before condemnation. She believes authority without character can become dangerous, vision without execution remains fantasy, and influence without service ultimately becomes empty. “Leadership begins where self-interest ends,” she says.

Building Enterprise as Infrastructure

The organisations led by SHEKINAIH represent different dimensions of what she describes as one wider stewardship.

“I want the testimony to be, ‘Look how people returned to their purpose and became restorers themselves.”

Page 14

CE Global Network (International) serves as the commercial and operational side of the ecosystem, with activities centred on disciplined execution, customer service, governance and value creation. Crowned Eagles Global represents the teaching, training, publishing, humanitarian and mission-oriented dimensions of her work.

The wider corporate structure across Singapore and Malaysia has been developed to create capacity in areas including business operations, intellectual property, publishing and long-term development. Yet SHEKINAIH does not view enterprise as an end in itself.

“I do not see enterprise as the throne. Enterprise is infrastructure,” she says.

This philosophy influences her understanding of capital, systems, intellectual property, logistics and organisational structures. These elements can become tools for preparedness, publishing, training, humanitarian assistance and long-term resilience. Rather than measuring enterprise only through accumulation, she believes leaders should consider what resources are ultimately intended to accomplish.

Her End-Time Economic Framework brings together principles of stewardship, diversification, resilience, liquidity, productive enterprise, intellectual-property protection and long-term institutional continuity. She sees a need to balance spiritual conviction with practical financial discipline. In her view, purpose without financial discipline can become unsustainable idealism, while wealth without purpose can become another form of bondage.

When Strategy Becomes Restoration

Although SHEKINAIH’s professional journey includes business development, international leadership and publishing, she believes the most meaningful measure of success is restoration.

“Leadership begins where self-interest ends.”

One personal experience continues to shape her understanding of leadership. She describes supporting a young man and his family through education, housing and food assistance and eventually using her savings to help establish a tuition centre that could provide a sustainable source of income.

The experience occurred during a particularly difficult period for her own family, yet it reinforced a lesson that remains central to her work. “The true measure of achievement is not what comes into our hands; it is what passes through our hands to restore another life,” she says.

That philosophy now influences her approach to enterprise, publishing, training and humanitarian work. She does not want organisations that simply produce impressive numbers. She wants structures capable of recognising and responding to human need.

“Restoration is where strategy becomes love in action,” she says.

For her, this idea connects leadership with responsibility. Business structures, financial resources and professional expertise become meaningful when they can be directed towards restoring lives and creating opportunities for others.

A Voice Beyond the Boardroom

Alongside her corporate leadership, SHEKINAIH has developed an extensive body of creative, educational and faith-based work. Her portfolio includes the five-book BEHOLD MY MESSENGER series, three related music albums and more than 200 teaching videos.

Her books and teachings represent a substantial part of her wider intellectual and spiritual body of work. She sees writing as more than creative expression. For her, it is a means of documenting ideas, preserving frameworks and creating material that can continue educating future generations.

Her completed editorial profile makes an important distinction regarding authorship. Siew Sin is presented as the Principal/Lead Author, while J Aaron K David is identified as Co-Author and Scriptural/Theological Contributor where applicable.

Page 16

Her creative work, business leadership and teaching activities are therefore connected by a larger objective: building systems and bodies of work capable of continuing beyond one individual. The emphasis is not only on creating something today but on ensuring that knowledge, structures and purpose remain useful in the future.

A Global Vision Built for Continuity

Looking ahead, Siew Sin envisions continued development across Singapore, Malaysia and international platforms. Her priorities include strengthening commercial capabilities, expanding publishing and teaching, protecting intellectual property, building humanitarian capacity and developing institutions capable of serving future generations.

She also carries what she describes as a 13-year generational vision. The objective is not to create followers who simply repeat the words of a founder. Instead, she wants to develop people who can think, lead, govern, build, serve, document, execute, restore and eventually train others.

“The greatest evidence of succession is not that people keep quoting the founder,” she says. “It is that they can faithfully continue the assignment.”

Her ultimate vision is therefore not centred on creating a monument to herself. She hopes the organisations, books, teachings and initiatives associated with her work will remain useful long after her direct involvement.

“I do not want history merely to say, ‘Look what I accomplished,’” she explains. “I want the testimony to be, ‘Look how people returned to their purpose and became restorers themselves.’”

Page 17

This approach places continuity at the centre of leadership. For her, the true measure of succession is the ability of others to carry responsibility with understanding, discipline and purpose.

Recognition Along the Journey

SHEKINAIH's profile also highlights a growing list of business, leadership and international recognitions. Among the achievements and recognitions identified for editorial consideration are the Singapore SME 500 Award associated with CE Global Network LLP, the Dun & Bradstreet Business Eminence Awards 2026 Silver Category associated with CE Global Network (International), the Global Women of Influence Awards 2026, and a range of international leadership, peace and humanitarian recognitions.

For her, however, recognition is not the ultimate measure of achievement. Awards may acknowledge a journey, but she believes they should never replace the purpose behind the work. The value of recognition lies in what it represents, while the deeper responsibility remains focused on stewardship, service and restoration.

The Mission Before the Structure

Perhaps the most defining message of SHEKINAIH's story is the reversal of the traditional entrepreneurial narrative. She did not, as she tells it, build a business and then search for meaning. The mission came first. The enterprise followed.

She was trained in finance but says her defining education came through sacrifice. She built companies but does not worship enterprise. She writes books, teaches internationally and receives recognition, yet insists that these things are instruments rather than destinations.

Page 18

“The true measure of achievement is not what comes into our hands; it is what passes through our hands to restore another life.”

Her story brings together professional competence, leadership, humanitarian responsibility and deeply held conviction. Each area contributes to a wider purpose of building institutions that can serve people and continue beyond an individual career.

For her, success is not defined simply by what a person builds. It is defined by what that person is willing to build for others. Enterprise provides the structure, leadership provides direction, resources provide capacity, and restoration gives the work its deeper meaning.

As she continues to expand her organisations, publishing, teaching and international initiatives, one sentence continues to define the philosophy behind her journey: “The mission came first, and enterprise became its earthly infrastructure.”

That principle captures the foundation of her approach to leadership. Her ambition is not simply to create larger organisations or accumulate greater recognition. It is to establish structures that can carry purpose, preserve knowledge, develop people and respond to human need.

In that vision, institutions are not monuments to their founders. They are vessels through which others can learn, lead, serve and restore. Her work ultimately reflects a belief that leadership becomes meaningful when it moves beyond personal achievement and creates the capacity for others to fulfil their own purpose.
